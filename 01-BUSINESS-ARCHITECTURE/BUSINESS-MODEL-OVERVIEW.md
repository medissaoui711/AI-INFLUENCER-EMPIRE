# Business Model Overview

AIE combines three related business arms. The intended relationship is a learning loop: owned media can test production and audience methods; agency work may expose client needs; systems can capture repeatable methods. Sharing learning does not mean that rights, client data, or assets can be reused without permission.

| Arm | Purpose and audience | Potential value / revenue hypotheses | Capabilities and dependencies | Validation and risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI Influencer Media | Develop and operate owned virtual influencer properties for audiences and potential brand partners. | Audience-supported media, sponsorships, licensing, affiliate activity, or owned offers are hypotheses only. | Character and editorial development, production, distribution, analytics, rights management; depends on platforms and audience response. | Test niche demand, content quality, distribution, rights, disclosure, and repeatability. Risks include platform dependence, IP disputes, and production cost. |
| AI Influencer Agency | Provide virtual influencer creation, management, or related services to external clients. | Project or retainer fees are hypotheses only. | Discovery, creative production, client management, approvals, delivery, contracting; depends on demonstrable capability and client trust. | Validate a real customer problem, willingness to pay, delivery cost, legal scope, and capacity. Risks include scope creep and unclear rights. |
| AI Influencer Systems | Develop reusable workflows, frameworks, templates, and IP for internal or external use. | Templates, training, licensing, or other productized systems are hypotheses only. | Process design, documentation, testing, version control, support and IP protection. | Validate usability, differentiation, demand, maintainability, and rights. Risks include premature productization and unsupported claims. |

The arms are strategic components, not currently staffed divisions. No customer, revenue, or operating capability is asserted as established.
